Eight stages. Many specialists. One finished shawl.

This sequence is a general educational orientation. It describes broad movements in the work, while workshop practice varies by place, maker, material, and object.

The journey of a shawl

The stages are kept in order to make the route legible. They are not a schedule or a record of any particular workshop.

  1. 01

    Collecting the fibre

    Pashm is gathered from the fine undercoat of pashmina-producing goats during seasonal shedding. The coarse outer hair and fine down are still mixed at this point.

  2. 02

    Cleaning & sorting

    The raw fibre is opened and sorted so coarse hair, dust, and plant matter can be separated from the fine down prepared for spinning.

  3. 03

    Hand spinning

    A spinner draws the cleaned fibre into yarn and controls its twist by touch. A yinder is one traditional hand-operated wheel associated with this work.

  4. 04

    Dyeing

    Yarn may be dyed before weaving or before it is used for surface work. Colour preparation depends on the intended textile and the practice of the makers involved.

  5. 05

    Weaving

    Warp and weft are brought together on a handloom. In Kani tapestry weaving, small kanis carry coloured wefts while a talim guides the pattern sequence.

  6. 06

    Surface embroidery

    When embroidery is part of the textile, it is worked after the base cloth is woven. Sozni uses a fine needle to build surface motifs that may include the curved boteh form.

  7. 07

    Washing & finishing

    The cloth is washed, dried, and finished according to the textile and local practice. A purzgar is a specialist associated with cleaning and refining the woven surface.

  8. 08

    Final inspection

    The finished textile is opened and checked across its surface and edges. Any further correction depends on the object, its condition, and the standards of the makers involved.

Read this sequence as a route through related skills, not as proof of how any specific textile was made.

Four terms, four defining characteristics

Technique and material are different kinds of information. The references below keep each museum record separate from the broader term it helps explain.

Pattern is formed through tapestry weaving with small wooden kanis.

Kani

Kani pattern is made as the textile is woven. Coloured wefts are carried with small wooden kanis, while a talim provides coded guidance for the design. The museum object shown here is related through its tapestry-weave structure, but The Met does not label it as a Kani shawl.

Fine surface embroidery is applied after the base cloth is woven.

Sozni

Sozni uses a needle and separate thread to form pattern on woven cloth. The museum object shown here is recorded as embroidered on the surface, but The Met does not identify it as Sozni. It therefore serves as a related surface-embroidery reference only.

Rich pattern is distributed across substantial areas of the cloth.

Jamawar

Jamawar refers to richly patterned shawl textiles. Construction should be read from the individual object record rather than inferred from the name alone. The Met explicitly identifies the museum object shown here as jamawar.

Fine pashm fibre provides the material identity of the textile.

Pashmina

Pashmina names fine pashm fibre and textiles made from it. Fibre identity is distinct from decorative technique: pashmina cloth may be plain, tapestry woven, or embroidered. The Met records pashmina wool in the medium of the museum object shown here.

Glossary

pashm
Fine undercoat fibre gathered from pashmina-producing goats.
kani
Lowercase kani names the small wooden bobbin tool used to carry coloured weft; uppercase Kani names the tapestry-weaving tradition.
talim
A coded pattern notation used to guide Kani weaving.
yinder
A hand-operated spinning wheel used to draw pashm into yarn.
Sozni
Sozni, also rendered Sozani in the Ministry source, is fine surface embroidery worked with a needle on woven cloth.
boteh
A curved plant form often rendered as a tapering leaf or cone.
purzgar
A specialist associated with cleaning and finishing a woven surface.
